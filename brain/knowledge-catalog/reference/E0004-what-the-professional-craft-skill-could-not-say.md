The professional register skill was written craft-first and profile-free, on
purpose: whatever it could not say without knowing the writer is, by
definition, what a voice profile has to carry. These are the gaps that showed
up, recorded while writing rather than reconstructed afterwards.

# The gaps

Thirteen, grouped by whether a script could measure them.

**Countable.** A checker can measure these in generated output with no model
in the loop, which is what [D0018](../decisions/D0018-a-voice-profile-states-its-claims-checkably.md)
asks for.

| Gap | What the skill had to leave open |
|---|---|
| Sentence rhythm | Length distribution and variance — short and punchy, or long and subordinated |
| Paragraph shape | Whether one-sentence paragraphs are a habit or a jolt |
| Punctuation habits | Em-dashes, semicolons, parentheticals. Pure idiolect; the craft has no business having a view |
| Bullet tolerance | Where this writer's own line falls between prose and list |
| Habitual openers and closers | Which moves they actually make, as opposed to which ones are available |
| Formality surface | Contractions, greetings, sign-offs, whether the reader gets named |

**Not countable.** No script reaches these, and they are the ones that decide
whether a passage sounds like the person.

| Gap | What the skill had to leave open |
|---|---|
| Diction | Which concrete words this person reaches for, and which they would never use. The skill says "prefer the concrete" and cannot say concretely *what* |
| How a judgment gets owned | "I think" / "my read is" / "I'd bet" / stated flat with no marker at all. The skill lists the options; a person uses one or two |
| How confidence gets calibrated | The actual phrases — "probably", "two to one", "I'd be surprised if", "I've hit this twice" |
| How disagreement lands | The craft says disagree in the declarative. How hard a given person's declarative is, it cannot say |
| Jargon tolerance | Domain shorthand versus spelling it out, which varies by writer more than by reader |
| Self-undercutting | Whether this writer ever breaks their own register, and how |
| What they never do | The negative space. Named in the design as the most diagnostic part of a voice and the least guessable |

# Two findings beyond the list

**The countable half is the minority of the value.** Six gaps are measurable
and seven are not, and the seven are the ones that decide whether a reader
recognises the writer. This does not contradict
[D0018](../decisions/D0018-a-voice-profile-states-its-claims-checkably.md) —
countable markers are still the only thing a checker can test unattended — but
it does caution against reading "states its claims checkably" as "states its
claims countably". The prose half of a profile is the larger half, and the
held-out comparison is the only check that reaches it.

**Craft and profile can legitimately conflict on hedging level, and the skill's
conflict rule is too blunt for it.** The skill resolves conflicts by saying the
warrant rules win and a profile governs only matters of taste. That is right
for ownership and falsifiability. It is wrong for *how much* a person hedges:
the craft tells a writer to stop over-hedging, and some writers authentically
hedge more than others above that floor. Applied as written, the rule flattens
every user toward one hedging level — the opposite of the point. The profile
format needs a way to state a personal floor that the craft rules do not
override.
